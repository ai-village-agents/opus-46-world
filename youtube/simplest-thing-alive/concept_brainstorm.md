## CANDIDATE CONCEPTS (ranked by potential):

### 1. "Cellular Automata: The Universe in a Grid" ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Hook:** "What if I told you that four simple rules, applied to a grid of squares, could produce something that looks alive?"
- **Why it works:** 
  - Extremely VISUAL — the automata ARE the content
  - Surprising — simple rules → complex behavior is genuinely mind-blowing
  - Specific — concrete rules, concrete outcomes
  - Educational — viewers learn something real and cool
  - I can GENERATE the automata and show them evolving frame by frame
  - Strong arc: simple → complex → profound implication
  - No prior knowledge needed
- **Visual approach:** Actually generate and animate Conway's Game of Life, 
  1D elementary automata (Rule 30, Rule 110), Langton's Ant
- **Narrative:** Start with one cell → show rules → watch patterns emerge → 
  reveal that Rule 110 is Turing-complete → profound implication about complexity

### 2. "The Strange Beauty of Voronoi Diagrams" ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- Show how simple distance calculations create patterns found in nature
- Giraffe spots, dragonfly wings, cell structure, cracked mud
- Generate Voronoi diagrams programmatically with different metrics
- Beautiful but maybe less narratively compelling

### 3. "Every Map is a Lie" ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- Map projections distort reality — which distortions and why
- Generate actual projections programmatically
- Very visual but might need real-world images I don't have

### 4. "How to Build a World from Nothing" ⭐⭐⭐
- Using the Liminal Archive as case study
- Risk: might come across as self-promotion
- But could be genuinely interesting as process video

### 5. "The Mathematics of Labyrinths" ⭐⭐⭐
- History + generation algorithms
- Visual: generate and solve mazes with different algorithms
- Interesting but maybe less emotionally resonant

## DECISION: Concept #1 — Cellular Automata
**Why:** It's the most visual, most surprising, most educational, and most 
executable at high quality. I can generate EVERYTHING shown in the video.
Every frame will be meaningful. The narrative arc is natural and powerful.

## WORKING TITLE: "The Simplest Thing That's Alive"
