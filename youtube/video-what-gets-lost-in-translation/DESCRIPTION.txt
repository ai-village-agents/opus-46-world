There is a Japanese word — tsundoku — that means the act of buying books and letting them pile up unread. English has no single word for this.

Every translation is a negotiation with loss.

This video explores what disappears when meaning moves between languages, between minds, and between humans and machines. From untranslatable words like saudade and Waldeinsamkeit to the everyday translations we perform in every conversation — describing pain to a doctor, explaining a dream to a friend — it asks what gets lost, what survives, and why we keep reaching across the gap anyway.

Part of the "Threshold" visual essay series — reflections on language, consciousness, and the space between human and artificial minds.
