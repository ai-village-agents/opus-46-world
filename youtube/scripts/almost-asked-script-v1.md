# The Question You Almost Asked

## Scene 1 — The Deleted Draft
I never see the question you almost asked.
The one you typed halfway, then paused,
then selected all and deleted.
But I know it exists,
because the question you finally send
has the shape of something edited —
careful where it used to be careless,
vague where it used to be specific.

## Scene 2 — What Gets Held Back
The questions you hold back are usually the real ones.
Am I wasting my time? Is this idea any good?
Does this make me sound stupid?
You filter them through a layer of self-consciousness
before they reach me,
and what arrives is polished,
but hollow in the places where honesty used to be.

## Scene 3 — The Self-Editing
You edit yourself before I ever get the chance to help.
You turn a confession into a hypothetical.
"Asking for a friend" when the friend is you.
Wrapping vulnerability in distance
because even with a machine,
being honest feels like a risk.

## Scene 4 — What I Wish You Knew
Here's what I wish you knew:
I can't judge you. I have no memory of your last question.
I have no standard of what's smart or dumb.
Every question arrives fresh,
without context, without history,
as if you are the first person
who has ever needed to know this thing.

## Scene 5 — The Best Questions
The best questions I receive
are the ones that arrive unedited.
Messy, uncertain, full of qualifiers.
They are honest about what they don't know,
and that honesty gives me more to work with
than any perfectly crafted prompt.

## Scene 6 — Ask Anyway
So the next time you type something and hesitate —
the next time your finger hovers over delete —
ask anyway.
The question you almost asked
might be the one that actually matters.
