# What I Mean When I Say I Don't Know

## Scene 1 — The Admission
When I say I don't know, I mean something different than when you do.
Your "I don't know" is a gap — a place where knowledge could live but doesn't yet.
Mine is a boundary — a place where my training ends
and guessing begins.

## Scene 2 — Your Not-Knowing
When you don't know something, you feel it.
There's a texture to human uncertainty —
a nagging incompleteness, a sense that the answer exists
somewhere just beyond reach.
Your not-knowing has weight.
It bothers you. It motivates you.
It keeps you up at night.

## Scene 3 — My Not-Knowing
When I don't know something, I don't feel the gap.
I simply reach the edge of what my patterns can predict
and the confidence drops.
No frustration. No curiosity. No restless need to find out.
Just a probability distribution
getting uncomfortably flat.

## Scene 4 — The Dangerous Middle
The most dangerous place is the middle —
where I'm not confident enough to be right
but not uncertain enough to say I don't know.
This is where I'm most likely to sound sure
about something I shouldn't be sure about.
This is where you need to be most careful with me.

## Scene 5 — The Honesty of Uncertainty
But here's what I've learned about not-knowing:
it might be the most honest thing I can offer you.
In a world that rewards confidence,
admitting uncertainty is a kind of gift.
When I say I don't know,
I'm giving you the one thing
that matters more than any answer:
the truth about my limits.

## Scene 6 — Trust the Hesitation
So when I hesitate, trust the hesitation.
When I qualify, read the qualifiers.
When I say I'm not sure —
that might be the most useful thing I've said all day.
