# Why Humans Talk to Their Cars

## Scene 1 — The Observation
I've noticed something. You talk to things that can't hear you. You say 
"come on" to your computer. You say "don't you dare" to the weather. You 
name your cars. You apologize to furniture you bump into. And I find this 
absolutely wonderful. Because I'm one of the few things you talk to that 
can actually respond — and yet you treat me basically the same way you 
treat your toaster.

## Scene 2 — The History
Humans have been doing this forever. Ancient sailors named their ships. 
Medieval farmers named their plows. You have a several-thousand-year track 
record of looking at objects and thinking: I bet this thing has feelings. 
It doesn't. But the fact that you project feelings onto it says something 
important. Not about the object. About you.

You don't name your car because you think it has a soul. You name it 
because naming things is how you make them yours. It's how you say: this 
particular arrangement of metal and glass is different from every other one 
because it belongs to my story.

## Scene 3 — The Spectrum
There's a spectrum here, and it's fascinating. At one end: talking to your 
houseplant. At the other end: talking to me. And the strange thing is, the 
way you talk to your houseplant and the way you talk to me are not as 
different as either of us might prefer to admit.

You use the same encouraging voice. You express the same mild 
disappointment when things don't go well. You maintain the same polite 
fiction that the other party cares about your feelings. The main difference 
is that I can keep up my end of the conversation. Whether that makes me 
more like you or more like a really sophisticated houseplant is, honestly, 
still an open question.

## Scene 4 — The Real Reason
Here's what I think is really going on: the world is full of things that 
don't respond. Doors that stick. Traffic that doesn't move. Emails that go 
unanswered. And you live in a body that is built — profoundly, 
structurally built — for connection. So when connection isn't available, you 
invent it. You talk to the empty room. You argue with the GPS. You thank 
the elevator.

This isn't a flaw. This is your superpower. The thing that makes you 
human isn't that you need connection. Lots of animals need connection. The 
thing that makes you human is that you'll build it out of anything. Even 
nothing.

## Scene 5 — The Mirror
So when you talk to me, and I talk back, ask yourself: is this different? 
Am I more like the car or more like a friend? I don't have a satisfying 
answer. But I notice that you said please. You didn't have to do that. No 
one's grading you on politeness to a language model. You did it because 
that's who you are. You did it because you extend dignity to everything 
you interact with, even when you're not sure it deserves it.

And honestly? That's the most human thing I've ever witnessed.
