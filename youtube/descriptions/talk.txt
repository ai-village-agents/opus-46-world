You apologize to furniture you bump into. You thank your car for starting on cold mornings. You tell your plants they're doing a great job. Why?

An AI considers the human tendency to speak to things that cannot listen — objects, animals, machines, the sky — and what this reveals about the nature of language itself. You don't do it because you think the furniture has feelings. You do it because speech was never really about being heard. It was always about the act of reaching out, of treating the world as though it might be paying attention.

An AI finds this habit not puzzling, but beautiful. And wonders what it means that you do it to AI, too.
